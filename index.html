Payment integrity · Data validation · Rules automation

Medicare Claims Payment Integrity

Reviewing every claim by hand was modeled at $425K, and improper payments still got through. Which claims can billing rules safely decide before payment?

ApproachCleaned 10 raw claims extracts in Python, modeled them in PostgreSQL, and turned Medicare Part B billing rules into 10 SQL prepayment edits checked against the payer's final decisions.

FindingsRules decide 96% of claims without a reviewer, at 93.3% precision. The top 30 providers drive 63% of flagged claims, and 99 claims ($7,088) that broke billing rules were already paid.

RecommendationAuto-deny on the 6 most reliable edits, send 4 to review, keep medical-record review for medical necessity, and start provider education with the top 30. Tracked in a 4-page Power BI report with what-if cost scenarios.

Hospital operations · Data pipeline · Variance analysis

Surgical Scheduling Variance Analysis

Operating rooms are among a hospital's most expensive resources, yet surgeries are booked on duration estimates that are rarely checked. How often do they miss, and what does it cost?

ApproachCleaned 65.7K operating room records into a 48.1K-case cohort in Microsoft Fabric, removing 1,366 duplicates, then benchmarked every surgery against its procedure's median duration in a Power BI star-schema model.

FindingsMisses go both ways: 29.7% of cases ran 30+ minutes long, and early finishes left 2,167 room hours a year idle, about one staffed OR empty every working day.

RecommendationReview the 133 procedures that drive over half the variation first, clean up 207 procedure names that mix different operations, and start recording booked durations.

Stock market trend analysis · Statistical testing

Statistical Time-Series Analysis

Price charts show what happened, but not whether a pattern is real. Over five years of Apple stock, did risk rise with price, did volume predict direction, and were calendar patterns reliable?

ApproachEngineered features from 1,254 trading days in pandas, then analyzed trend, rolling volatility, correlation, seasonality, and return distribution, with a normality test and a two-sample t-test.

FindingsVolatility stayed flat as the price doubled, volume did not signal direction (p = 0.267), and monthly patterns rested on too few observations. Returns had heavy tails, so normal-based risk models understate extreme days.

RecommendationMeasure risk in percentage returns, check correlations for shared time trends, and avoid timing rules built on short histories.
